Part 2 · The G1 as a bandit

When to brake against a push

The cue (situation)

Every trial a tone announces a push from behind, 250 N for 0.1 s, and says when:

soon: 0.2 s after the cue
late: 0.5 s after the cue

The moves (actions)

brake early: walk 0.4 m/s slower, 0.06 to 0.3 s after the cue

brake late: the same, 0.36 to 0.6 s

keep walking

The reward

3 s after the push: 1 if the robot is still up, 0 if it fell.

Nothing about the push, its timing, or which way the fall went.

Each trial starts at a random point of the stride, so the same move does not always work: the world pays with a probability.

Learning from reward · 6

Where the moves come from: week 16's trained brace, logged around a push, brakes hard (vx -0.39) exactly while the push lands, then speeds up. Here the brake's shape is given; the striatum only chooses its timing. A trial resets the body, walks 2 s plus a random 0-39 ticks, then cues.
