Part 1 · People do this too

The after-effect is the proof of learning

Prism glasses

Glasses shift what you see 10° to the side. Your throws miss, then land again within a few dozen throws. Take the glasses off: you miss to the other side.

Reaching in a force field

A robot arm pushes your hand sideways as you reach. The reach curves, then straightens. Switch the field off: it curves the opposite way.

A split-belt treadmill

One leg on a faster belt: you limp, then walk evenly. Back on one speed, you limp the other way for a while.

Reacting to an error cannot do this: when the push is gone, a reflex has nothing to react to. A mirror-image error after the push means a correction was learned and is still being sent.

Classic findings: prism adaptation (Helmholtz; Martin et al. 1996, which also needs the cerebellum), force-field reaching (Shadmehr and Mussa-Ivaldi 1994), split-belt walking (Reisman et al. 2005). The point to land: an after-effect separates feed-forward learning from feedback reaction. The robot will show one.
