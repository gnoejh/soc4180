Part 1 · Principles

What the cerebellum does

It predicts what a movement will do to the body, and times actions to what is about to happen.

Most of the brain’s neurons

About four in five neurons sit in the cerebellum, nearly all of them tiny granule cells.

Damage: clumsy, not paralysed

Movements still happen, but overshoot, wobble and come at the wrong moment (ataxia).

It learns from mistakes

One dedicated wire per cell reports the error; the cell corrects itself.

The cerebellum in a walking robot · 3

Two jobs: prediction (the forward model) and timing (learned anticipation). Both are learned from error, online, without a training set. Ask: why would a body need to predict itself? Answer to draw out: sensing is slow, so acting on the senses alone is always late.
