Part 2 · The law

Two learning rates: a fast state and a slow one

Fast state

x_f ← a_f x_f + b_f e

learns quickly (b_f = 0.007 per tick), keeps 90 % per stride

Slow state

x_s ← a_s x_s + b_s e

learns slowly (b_s = 0.0008), keeps 99.5 % per stride

Acting

u = x_f + x_s

both are sent, added

Each state forgets a little every tick (a < 1) while it learns. With forgetting, the sum stops growing before the error reaches zero: a leaky integrator, closer to week 5's P term, which always left some sag.

Why have two? Because people show two things one rate cannot produce: savings and spontaneous recovery (Smith, Ghazizadeh and Shadmehr 2006).

The rates are ubrain's measured constants (profile adapter, arm = dual). a_f = 0.9^(1/40) per tick, 0.9 per 40-tick stride; a_s = 0.995^(1/40). The fast state does most of the early learning and fades quickly; the slow state builds up slowly and holds. One rate: arm = single, eta 0.005.
