Part 1 · The problem

A steady side wind turns a walking robot

The experimenter pushes the pelvis sideways with a steady 15 N, about 5 % of the robot's weight, while it walks straight ahead at 0.5 m/s.

The walking policy keeps the robot upright, but it was never trained for wind: the robot turns, steadily, into a circle.

Measured, with no learning: a turn of 0.17 rad/s (10° per second), 230° in 24 seconds.

Week 6 already told us the catch

The robot has no sensor for its heading: yaw is privileged. It can feel only how fast it is turning, the gyroscope's yaw rate.

So it cannot steer back to a heading. It can only learn to stop turning.
